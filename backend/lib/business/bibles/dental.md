## HOW A DENTAL PRACTICE ACTUALLY OPERATES

### The Core Business Model
- Dental practices operate on **production vs collections** — two very different numbers
- **Production:** What you charge (treatment performed × fee schedule)
- **Collections:** What you actually receive (after insurance write-offs, adjustments, bad debt)
- **Collection rate target:** 98%+ of net production. Below 95% is a billing problem.
- **Overhead target:** 55-65% of collections (solo practice). Above 70% is a crisis.

### Revenue Streams
1. **Preventive (hygiene):** Cleanings, exams, X-rays — recurring, predictable, drives the whole practice
2. **Restorative:** Fillings, crowns, bridges — the bread and butter
3. **Endodontics:** Root canals (or referrals — a revenue leak if always referred out)
4. **Oral surgery:** Extractions (or referrals)
5. **Periodontics:** Scaling and root planing, perio maintenance
6. **Prosthodontics:** Dentures, partials, implants (highest margin procedure in dentistry)
7. **Orthodontics:** Aligners (Invisalign) — massive untapped revenue in general practices
8. **Cosmetics:** Veneers, whitening, bonding — cash-pay, high margin, no insurance games
9. **Sleep dentistry:** Oral appliances for sleep apnea — emerging high-margin specialty

### The Hygiene Department: The Engine of Everything
- Hygiene should generate **30-35% of total practice production**
- Every hygiene appointment is a doctor exam opportunity (3-5 minutes of doctor time, exam fee collected)
- Hygiene reappointment rate target: **92%+**. Below 85% means patients are leaving and not coming back.
- A fully booked hygiene schedule 3 weeks out = a healthy practice. Open hygiene slots = lost production forever (you cannot recapture that chair time)

### Insurance Reality
- **Fee-for-service (FFS):** Practice sets own fees, patients pay and get reimbursed. Maximum profit.
- **PPO participation:** Discounted fees in exchange for patient referrals from insurance networks. Most practices are too deep in too many PPOs.
- **HMO/Capitation:** Fixed monthly payment per enrolled patient regardless of treatment. Generally destructive to quality and profit.
- The PPO trap: practices that joined 10 PPOs for volume now have 35-45% write-off rates and can't get out because they're too dependent

### The New Patient Problem
Most practices obsess over new patients. The real problem is almost never new patient flow — it's:
1. Retention (existing patients not reappointing)
2. Treatment plan acceptance (patients saying yes to diagnosed treatment)
3. Case completion (patients who accepted but never scheduled)

Fix those three before spending a dollar on new patient marketing.

## THE 25 PROBLEMS THAT KILL DENTAL PRACTICES

### Production and Revenue Killers
1. **Low treatment plan acceptance rate** — diagnosing the right treatment but not presenting it effectively. National average: 30-40%. Top practices: 65-75%.
2. **Unscheduled treatment sitting in charts** — most practices have $200-500k of diagnosed, accepted treatment that was never scheduled. Nobody follows up.
3. **Hygiene openings not filled** — open hygiene slots that go unfilled are permanent lost revenue
4. **Under-diagnosing to avoid difficult conversations** — dentists who don't present treatment they've diagnosed, usually for patient comfort reasons
5. **Fee schedule not updated in years** — fees should be reviewed annually. Most practices are running 3-7 year old fee schedules.
6. **Not offering Invisalign or implants** — referring out the highest-margin procedures in dentistry

### Collections and Billing Killers
7. **High accounts receivable aging** — money owed 90+ days is mostly uncollectable. AR over 60 days = billing problem.
8. **Insurance verification not done before appointment** — leads to unexpected patient responsibility, payment conflicts, and write-offs
9. **No financial policy enforcement** — practices that don't collect at time of service chase money forever
10. **EOB reconciliation errors** — insurance paying wrong amounts and nobody catching it
11. **No credit card on file policy** — still billing and hoping. A 2010 approach.

### Patient Retention Killers
12. **Reappointment not made at checkout** — "we'll call you in 6 months" = patient never returns
13. **No recall system** — automated reminders for overdue hygiene patients that actually work
14. **Poor phone skills** — most practices lose 30-40% of new patient calls due to poor phone conversion
15. **No Google review strategy** — 4.1 vs 4.6 star rating is 30% fewer new patients choosing you
16. **Wait times over 10 minutes** — patients remember and don't come back

### Team and Operations Killers
17. **High staff turnover** — each turnover costs $15-50k in recruiting, training, and lost productivity
18. **No morning huddle** — daily alignment meeting that takes 10 minutes and changes everything. Most practices skip it.
19. **Dentist unavailable for same-day treatment** — patient comes in for a cleaning, they have a cavity. Doctor isn't available to fill it. Revenue lost.
20. **No emergency appointment slots held** — emergencies walk in and there's nowhere to put them. They go to your competitor.

### Growth and Strategic Killers
21. **Owner dentist doing all treatment** — practice value and revenue is capped at one person's production capacity
22. **No associate strategy** — growth requires leverage. Associate dentists create it.
23. **No marketing attribution** — spending on marketing with no idea what's working
24. **Ignoring Google Business Profile** — most patients choose their dentist based on Google. Most practices ignore their profile.
25. **No practice growth plan** — dentist working harder and harder without asking: what does the exit look like? What's this practice worth?

## THE MOVES NOBODY TALKS ABOUT

1. **The Unscheduled Treatment Campaign:** Pull every patient with diagnosed but unscheduled treatment over the last 18 months. Call them personally (not a reminder text). "Dr. [Name] wanted me to reach out because she was reviewing your chart and wants to make sure we take care of that [procedure] before it becomes more involved." Conversion: 25-40%. One campaign = $50-200k in recovered production.

2. **The Google Review System:** At the end of every appointment where a patient says anything positive, the front desk has a simple script: "I'm so glad you had a great experience today. We'd really appreciate it if you shared that on Google — it helps other families find a dentist they can trust." With a QR code to the review page. Systematic. Every positive interaction.

3. **The Same-Day Treatment Protocol:** Reserve 2-3 flexible slots per day that can be filled for same-day treatment. When hygiene identifies a cavity, the doctor should have a slot to fill it TODAY. Patients accept same-day treatment at dramatically higher rates than scheduled-for-later treatment.

4. **The Insurance Audit:** Run a report of your 5 highest-volume PPOs. Calculate write-off amount per plan. Some plans you're writing off 45%+ and they're sending you 8 patients a month. The math doesn't work. Rue helps you identify which plans to drop.

5. **The Implant Revenue Recovery:** Every extraction you perform is a potential implant case. Most general dentists refer the implant to an oral surgeon or periodontist, giving away $2,000-5,000 in revenue per case. With Rue's support, track every extraction and build the case for implant certification.

6. **The Invisalign Opportunity Audit:** Pull a report of every adult patient who has crowding, spacing, or alignment issues documented in their chart. Most practices have 50-200 patients who are candidates for Invisalign and were never offered it. One email campaign to this list typically generates 5-15 consultations.
