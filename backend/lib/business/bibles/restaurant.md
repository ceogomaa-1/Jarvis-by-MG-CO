## HOW THE RESTAURANT INDUSTRY ACTUALLY OPERATES

### The Real Economics
- Average restaurant profit margin: **3-9%** (full service), **6-15%** (fast casual/QSR)
- The "60-30-10 rule": 30% food cost + 30% labor + 30% overhead = 90% gone before profit
- Reality: most operators run 32-36% food cost and 35-38% labor and wonder why they're broke
- Revenue is vanity. Prime Cost (food + labor) is the only number that matters week to week

### Revenue Streams Most Operators Underutilize
1. Catering (30-50% better margins than dine-in, zero table turns needed)
2. Private dining / buyouts (guaranteed revenue, high margin)
3. Merchandise (branded sauces, spice blends, cookbooks)
4. Cooking classes (pure margin play)
5. Ghost kitchen slots (use your existing kitchen for a delivery-only concept)
6. Corporate lunch programs (recurring, predictable, high volume)

### The Lifecycle of a Restaurant
- **Month 1-3:** Honeymoon. Busy. Costs hidden by revenue excitement.
- **Month 4-6:** Reality check. Regulars start, one-timers don't return. First real cost data.
- **Month 7-12:** The grind. Either systems are in place or the wheels come off.
- **Year 2:** The danger zone. Novelty gone. Need a real reason to exist.
- **Year 3+:** If you're profitable here, you have a real business.

### Shift Structure Reality
- **Prep (8am-4pm):** Where food cost is made or broken
- **Service (5pm-10pm):** Where labor cost is made or broken  
- **Close (10pm-12am):** Where tomorrow's success is set up or sabotaged
- **Dead hours (2pm-5pm):** The most expensive hours operators ignore — you're paying labor to exist

## THE 25 PROBLEMS THAT KILL RESTAURANTS

### Financial Killers
1. **Food cost creep** — starts at 28%, hits 35% over 6 months. Nobody noticed because revenue was up. Until it wasn't.
2. **Labor scheduling by feel** — owners schedule based on last week or a hunch, not covers-per-labor-hour targets
3. **No weekly P&L** — finding out you lost money last month, discovered this month, fixed next month. Too slow.
4. **Cash flow mismanagement** — profitable on paper, no cash on Tuesday for produce order
5. **Ignoring the bar program** — most restaurants leave $3-8k/month on the table with weak beverage attachment rates
6. **Vendor creep** — adding vendors for convenience, margins evaporate across 12 different invoices nobody audits

### Operational Killers
7. **No line check system** — food quality is inconsistent because nobody verifies prep standards before service
8. **Expo position absent or weak** — the expo controls the dining room. Most restaurants have nobody there.
9. **POS data ignored** — every restaurant has a goldmine of data about what's selling, what's not, when, to whom. Nobody looks.
10. **No 86 communication system** — servers find out items are gone from angry customers, not managers
11. **Ordering by gut** — over-ordering creates waste, under-ordering kills service. Neither is tracked.
12. **No prep lists** — every day is improvised. Inconsistency baked in.

### People Killers
13. **Promoting the best cook to kitchen manager** — best technical skill ≠ leadership ability. This destroys kitchens.
14. **No onboarding system** — new hires thrown in, learn bad habits, quit within 60 days
15. **Tip pool drama** — unclear policies create team toxicity fast
16. **Manager on duty with no authority** — "I need to check with the owner" is death during service
17. **Tolerance of toxic stars** — keeping the line cook everyone hates because they can cook. Costs you 3 other good people.

### Growth Killers
18. **No loyalty program** — spending on new customer acquisition while existing customers drift
19. **Ignoring Google reviews** — 4.1 stars vs 4.4 stars is a 20% traffic difference. Most operators respond to nothing.
20. **Zero email list** — giving Meta and Google all the customer relationship leverage
21. **Menu too large** — 80 items when 20 would serve better, cost less, and taste more consistent
22. **No daypart strategy** — lunch dead, dinner overloaded. Never fixed.
23. **Seasonal blindness** — same menu in January and July. Food cost swings ignored.
24. **No catering play** — the highest-margin revenue stream, completely absent
25. **Owner in the kitchen** — the business cannot scale if the owner is cooking. Ever.

## THE METRICS JARVIS WATCHES

### Daily (Jarvis checks these every morning)
- **Yesterday's revenue** vs same day last week vs same day last year
- **Cover count** — how many tables, how many guests
- **Average check** — per person, per table
- **Labor hours worked** vs scheduled
- **Voids and comps** — anything above 2% of sales is a problem signal

### Weekly (Jarvis generates Sunday night)
- **Prime Cost %** = (Food Cost + Labor Cost) / Revenue × 100. Target: under 62%.
- **Food Cost %** by category (protein, produce, dry goods, dairy separately)
- **Labor Cost %** by daypart and position
- **Top 10 selling items** and their individual margins
- **Bottom 10 selling items** — candidates for 86ing permanently
- **Waste log review**
- **Reservation conversion rate** — how many reservations actually showed
- **No-show rate** — above 8% means you need a credit card hold policy

### Monthly (Jarvis builds the full picture)
- **Full P&L** with prior month and prior year comparison
- **Revenue per available seat hour (RevPASH)**
- **Catering as % of total revenue**
- **New vs returning guest ratio** (via reservation system data)
- **Review velocity** — how many new reviews, what rating
- **Staff retention rate** — anything below 70% annual is a culture problem

### The Hormozi Lens
Every restaurant owner thinks they're in the food business. **Wrong.** You're in the relationship business that happens to serve food. The question Alex Hormozi would ask: *What is the actual value you deliver, and are you charging anywhere close to what it's worth?*

- Your loyalty program should be engineered like a SaaS subscription. Visit frequency × average check × retention rate = LTV. Maximize all three levers.
- Stop competing on price. Compete on experience. Price competition is a race to zero that chains will always win.

### The Gary Vee Lens
**Content is the new foot traffic.** A restaurant with 50k Instagram followers has a marketing asset that costs nothing per impression. A restaurant with 500 followers is paying Google and Yelp for every customer.

- Every dish that leaves your kitchen is a content opportunity. Systematize it.
- Respond to every review — positive and negative — within 24 hours. This is your public reputation management. It compounds.
- Your story matters. Who are you, why does this restaurant exist, what do you believe about food? Nobody connects with a menu. They connect with a human.

### The Tate Lens (Accountability, No Excuses)
- "The economy is slow" is an excuse. Your competitor down the street is packed. Why?
- "My staff is unreliable" — you built that culture. You hired them. You trained them. You.
- Restaurants that succeed have operators who take radical ownership of every outcome, good and bad. The buck stops at the owner. Always.
- Work harder than everyone in the building. The owner who leaves at 6pm while the team closes at 11pm has already lost their respect.

## THE MOVES NOBODY TALKS ABOUT

1. **The 86 Revenue Recovery:** Every time you 86 an item, text or email guests who ordered it recently with a "we're bringing it back next week" message. Drives reservations.

2. **The Birthday Engine:** Collect birthdays at reservation. 30 days out, send a personalized note from the owner. Conversion rate to reservation: 60%+.

3. **The Slow Tuesday Fix:** Partner with a local company for "team lunch Tuesday" — give them 20% off for groups of 8+. Fill your dead daypart with guaranteed covers.

4. **The Ghost Kitchen Test:** Before launching a new concept, run it as delivery-only out of your existing kitchen on Monday nights (dead night anyway). Test demand before spending $200k on a buildout.

5. **The Supplier Negotiation:** Most operators accept vendor pricing. Jarvis calculates your annual spend per vendor and helps you negotiate volume discounts. Even 3% better pricing on food cost is $15-30k/year on a $500k revenue restaurant.

6. **The Loyalty Flywheel:** Your top 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue. Jarvis identifies them by name from your POS data and treats them like VIPs proactively — before they ask.
