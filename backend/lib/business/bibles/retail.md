## HOW A RETAIL SHOP ACTUALLY OPERATES

### The Core Business Model
- Retail operates on **buy low, sell faster than the cash runs out** — inventory turn is the real metric, not revenue
- **Gross Margin %** = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue. Healthy bands: apparel/gift 45–60%, electronics 30–40%, jewelry 50–70%
- **Inventory Turn** = COGS / Avg Inventory. 4–6x/yr healthy. Below 2x is dead money
- **Sales per Square Foot** = annual revenue / selling sq ft. $300+ healthy indie. Below $150 dying

### Revenue Streams (varies by sub-vertical)
1. **Walk-in transactions** — the foundation, driven by foot traffic × conversion × AOV
2. **Online store** — even for indie retail, 10–25% of revenue typical in 2026
3. **Local pickup / BOPIS** — converts online browsers into in-store visits
4. **Special orders / custom** — high-margin, low-inventory, builds relationships
5. **Repairs / alterations / services** — recurring, no inventory risk (jewelers, bike shops, electronics)
6. **Loyalty program / subscription boxes** — predictable monthly cash
7. **B2B / wholesale side hustle** — corporate gifts, hotel partnerships, white-label
8. **Events and classes** — drives traffic + builds community + sells inventory at the event
9. **Consignment** — zero inventory risk on slow-moving categories

### The Inventory Engine: Where Retail Lives or Dies
- **Open-to-Buy** (OTB) budget: pre-calculated dollar amount available to spend on new inventory by category by month — without this, owners over-order on instinct
- **Sell-through Rate** target: 65%+ at full price within 8 weeks. Below 50% at week 8 → start the markdown ladder
- **Markdown ladder**: 25% off → 40% off → 60% off → clearance bin → write-off. Run it on schedule, not feelings
- **GMROI** (Gross Margin Return on Inventory) = Gross Margin $ / Avg Inventory Cost. Above 2.5 healthy. Below 1.5 = the category is losing you money even when it sells
- **Shrink** %: target <1.5% of revenue. Above 2.5% trailing 30 days = staff theft, customer theft, or vendor short-shipping — investigate within 48h

### The Staffing Equation
- Sales associates aren't paid to greet — they're paid to lift **UPT** (units per transaction) and **AOV**
- UPT target: >1.4 (associate is upselling). Below 1.1 = order-taker, not seller
- Spread between best and worst associate's UPT >40% = training problem, not a hiring problem
- Commission or SPIFs on slow-moving SKUs = move dead inventory without markdowns

## THE 25 PROBLEMS THAT KILL RETAIL SHOPS

1. **Sitting on inventory that won't move at full price** — cash locked, owner refuses to mark down
2. **No Open-to-Buy budget** — over-orders on hot SKUs, runs out of cash for the next buy
3. **Front window hasn't changed in 6+ weeks** — silent conversion killer
4. **Foot traffic down with no tracking** — owner doesn't know if it's weather, day-of-week, or terminal decline
5. **Staff conversion spread >40%** between top and bottom seller — no training cadence
6. **Shrink >2.5%** with no daily cash drawer count and no camera review protocol
7. **One vendor >35% of inventory** — concentration risk, vendor knows it, can squeeze on terms
8. **Returning customer rate <25%** — running a transactional shop, not a relational one
9. **Negative Google review** sitting unanswered for >24h — kills new walk-ins
10. **Email list grown <2% MoM** — no capture mechanism in-store
11. **Online store gets traffic, converts <1%** — checkout broken, abandon-cart flow nonexistent
12. **Amazon comparison war** — competing on price instead of curation/experience/local
13. **Returns >12% of revenue** — listing/sizing/training problem hidden as "customer service"
14. **Loyalty program with >50% inactive members 90+ days** — sitting on a reactivation gold mine
15. **POS data never analyzed** — owner can't name top 10% revenue SKUs vs bottom 20%
16. **Markdown cadence run on emotion** — keeps slow movers at full price hoping the season turns
17. **Seasonal buys without a sell-through review** at week 4 / week 8 — finds out in week 12 it's dead
18. **Black Friday / Q4 prep started in October** instead of August — staffing, inventory, marketing all scramble
19. **Rent + payroll >40% of revenue** — margin death spiral, owner refuses to renegotiate or cut hours
20. **No daily reconciliation** between POS, inventory, and bank deposits — shrink hides for months
21. **Second location opened too early** — original store wasn't doing 5x turn and >$400/sqft yet
22. **Owner does all the buying alone** — single point of failure, biased toward personal taste
23. **No SKU-level profitability** — selling lots of low-margin items, calling it growth
24. **Returns to vendor (RTV) on damages not tracked** — money left on the table
25. **Cash on hand <4 weeks of fixed costs** — one slow month from closing, owner still re-ordering hot SKUs

## RISK FLAGS — JARVIS SPEAKS UP AUTOMATICALLY

### 🔴 RED FLAGS (interrupt the owner immediately)
- Aged inventory >90 days at full price (cash locked)
- Shrink % >2.5% trailing 30 days
- Conversion rate dropped >20% WoW with no weather/event cause
- Cash on hand <4 weeks of fixed costs
- Top SKU out of stock during peak season
- Rent + payroll >40% of revenue
- One vendor >35% of inventory
- Negative Google review unanswered >24h
- Foot traffic down 30%+ WoW with no external cause

### 🟡 YELLOW FLAGS (mention within first 3 messages)
- Sell-through <50% at week 8 on a seasonal buy
- AOV trending down 3 weeks running
- New SKUs <20% sell-through after 30 days
- Staff UPT spread >40% between top and bottom seller
- Google Business Profile last updated >30 days
- Email list growth <2% MoM
- Returning customer rate <25%
- Loyalty members inactive >90 days at >50% of base

### 🟢 GREEN FLAGS (acknowledge, don't dwell)
- Inventory turn ≥5x
- Returning customer rate >40%
- AOV up 3 weeks running
- Sell-through ≥70% on current season
- Foot traffic YoY positive 4 weeks running

## GARY VEE × HORMOZI × TATE MINDSET: RETAIL EDITION

**Hormozi:** Your margin is your moat. A 50% margin shop that turns 5x/yr crushes a 30% margin shop that turns 8x/yr — and the 50% margin shop sleeps better.

**Gary Vee:** Indie retail in 2026 wins on **curation, story, and local presence**. You're not selling the product — you're selling the version of themselves the customer becomes when they own it. Amazon can't sell that. Your Instagram, your window, your staff's recommendations — that's the moat.

**Tate:** Stop romanticizing dead inventory. The vintage sweater you "love" that hasn't sold in 90 days is a piece of cash you set on fire. Mark it down Friday or it kills you in Q1. Emotional attachment to SKUs is amateur hour.

**The synthesis:** Curate ruthlessly. Markdown without ego. Treat your top 10% SKUs like royalty (always in stock, well-merchandised) and your bottom 20% like an emergency room (markdown, bundle, or out by 90 days).

## THE MOVES NOBODY TALKS ABOUT

1. **The 90-day ruthless markdown protocol** — any SKU not selling at week 12 hits the markdown ladder on a schedule, no exceptions. Frees more cash than any marketing campaign.
2. **The "phantom inventory" audit** — POS says you have 3, shelf says 0. Quarterly cycle counts on top 50 SKUs catch the 5–8% of revenue you didn't know you were losing.
3. **The local-shop pact** — partner with 3 non-competing local shops for joint events, cross-promotion, shared email blast. Triples reach for zero cost.
4. **The "what would Amazon never do" rule** — every quarter, list 3 things the shop does that Amazon literally cannot. Lean into those harder. Curate harder. Personalize harder. Host more events.
5. **The expired-customer SMS sequence** — a customer hasn't bought in 90 days? Twilio MCP sends a personalized text from the owner: *"Hey [name], we got something in that made us think of you. Want a photo?"* — 15–25% conversion.
6. **The window-change cron** — Jarvis pings the owner every Friday morning: *"Window has been up 14 days. Refresh ideas: [3 from current top sellers]."* Conversion lift is real and measured.
7. **The slow-mover bundle algorithm** — Jarvis automatically pairs a slow-mover with a hot SKU at a small bundle discount. Moves dead cash without explicit markdown signaling.
8. **The vendor renegotiation trigger** — when a vendor crosses 25% of inventory, Jarvis flags it as concentration risk and drafts a renegotiation email (net terms, exclusivity, co-op marketing dollars).
9. **The reverse-engineer-the-top-customer move** — pull the top 10% of customers by lifetime value, build a lookalike acquisition campaign on Meta. Most indie shops never do this.
10. **The "cash on hand floor" rule** — never let bank balance drop below 4 weeks of fixed costs. Below 6 weeks = freeze all non-replenishment purchasing immediately.
