## HOW TRADES BUSINESSES ACTUALLY OPERATE

### The Business Models
- **Service/Repair:** HVAC tune-ups, emergency plumbing, electrical service calls — high frequency, lower ticket, immediate cash collection
- **Replacement:** Equipment replacement, full system installs — medium frequency, medium-high ticket
- **Renovation/Remodel:** Kitchen, bathroom, full home renovation — low frequency, very high ticket, long project cycles
- **New Construction:** Working with builders on new builds — volume-based, thin margins, payment terms often 30-60 days
- **Maintenance Contracts:** Recurring service agreements — the gold standard. Predictable revenue, highest LTV per customer.

### The Cash Flow Reality (The Most Misunderstood Aspect of Contracting)
- Most contractors are **profitable on paper and broke in practice**
- **Revenue ≠ Cash.** A $50,000 job invoiced is not $50,000 in the bank.
- **Float:** The gap between when you pay for materials and labor and when the client pays you
- **Retainage:** Clients (especially commercial) hold 5-10% of every payment until project completion. On a $500k job, that's $25-50k floating for months.
- **The WIP (Work In Progress) trap:** A contractor with 10 active jobs all 50% complete has massive receivables, massive payables, and zero cash. This kills good businesses.

### Estimating Reality
- Most contractors estimate off gut feel and history. This works until it doesn't.
- **True job cost = Materials + Labor + Overhead allocation + Profit margin**
- Most contractors forget overhead allocation. They price for materials and direct labor and wonder why they're not making money.
- **Overhead** includes: vehicles, insurance, office, phone, admin, marketing, equipment, shop
- A contractor with $2M revenue and $400k overhead needs to price $400k of overhead recovery into their jobs. Most price $200k in.

### Subcontractor Management
- Every subcontractor relationship is a risk. They can ghost, underperform, cause liability, or steal clients.
- Subs should be treated like partners but managed with contracts, COIs, and performance standards.
- Over-reliance on one sub for a critical trade is a single point of failure.

## THE 25 PROBLEMS THAT KILL TRADES BUSINESSES

### Cash Flow Killers
1. **No job costing** — pricing based on gut, never knowing true job profitability until it's too late
2. **Poor payment terms** — billing on completion instead of milestone payments. You're financing your client's project.
3. **No cash reserve** — one bad month or one customer not paying wipes out operating capital
4. **Retainage blindness** — not accounting for retainage in cash flow projections
5. **Material overspending** — buying materials without tracking against estimate. Costs creep.
6. **Equipment financing abuse** — every shiny truck and tool on a payment instead of buying with cash when profitable

### Estimating and Pricing Killers
7. **Not charging for overhead** — pricing covers materials and labor, overhead eaten from profit
8. **Estimating by competition** — pricing based on what you think competitors charge, not on your actual cost structure
9. **Scope creep unpaid** — client adds work, contractor does it, never charges for it. $5-20k given away per project.
10. **No change order process** — verbal change approvals, disputes at payment time
11. **Low-balling to win work** — winning jobs at break-even or loss to "keep the crew busy"

### Operations Killers
12. **No project management system** — jobs tracked in the owner's head. Missed deadlines, miscommunication, chaos.
13. **No daily job site reporting** — no visibility into what's happening when owner isn't on site
14. **Poor materials management** — wrong materials ordered, delayed delivery, jobs stalled waiting for product
15. **No subcontractor coordination system** — subs showing up at wrong times, holding up other trades
16. **Owner as bottleneck** — every decision requires the owner. Business cannot run without them.

### Business Development Killers
17. **No referral system** — best lead source (past client referrals) completely unmanaged
18. **No Google Business presence** — most homeowners find contractors on Google. Most contractors have no profile.
19. **Seasonal revenue gaps** — winter slowdowns that were predictable but not planned for
20. **Single client dependency** — one builder or property manager accounts for 40%+ of revenue
21. **No follow-up after estimate** — send a quote, never follow up. Close rate stays at 20%.

### People Killers
22. **Hiring in desperation** — hiring anyone with a pulse during busy season. Quality disasters follow.
23. **No safety culture** — OSHA violations, workers' comp claims, liability exposure
24. **Foreman without authority** — site leader who can't make decisions, everything escalates to owner
25. **Key man risk** — one lead technician or estimator holds the entire business in their head

- Cash position will go negative within 30 days based on projected inflows/outflows
- A job is 30+ days past original completion with no completion date established
- An invoice is 60+ days overdue with no payment plan in place
- A key employee resignation (especially foreman or lead estimator)
- A major client has gone quiet after a change order dispute

🟡 **WARNING:**
- Estimate close rate drops below 25% (pricing or presentation problem)
- Labor hours tracking 15%+ over budget on an active job (cost overrun coming)
- No new leads in 14 days (pipeline will dry up in 60-90 days)
- Cash reserve below 6 weeks of operating expenses
- Materials costs spiking — time to reprice all active estimates

- A major local project announced (commercial development, school renovation, infrastructure work)
- Slow season approaching — maintenance contract renewal push now
- A competitor went out of business or had a publicized quality issue (their clients are available)
- A supplier offering volume discount on materials needed for upcoming jobs

## GARY VEE × HORMOZI × TATE MINDSET: TRADES EDITION

### The Hormozi Lens
Most contractors compete on price because they haven't figured out how to differentiate on value.

- The Grand Slam Offer for a contractor: "We complete on schedule or we give you $500/day off the contract. We provide daily photo updates. We handle permits so you don't have to deal with city. And we give you a 5-year workmanship warranty." This is worth a 20-30% premium over competitors. Homeowners will pay it.
- Maintenance agreements are the SaaS model of contracting. $150/month for annual HVAC tune-up, priority emergency service, and 15% off all repairs. That customer is worth $5,000-15,000 over 5 years vs the one-time $300 service call customer.
- Stop bidding against three other contractors on the same job. If you're always in a bidding war, your marketing isn't generating inbound demand. Fix the marketing, stop begging for work.

### The Gary Vee Lens
The trades are the most underrepresented industry in social media content — which means it's a massive opportunity.

- "Day in the life of a contractor" content performs incredibly well. People are fascinated by the craft. A bathroom renovation time-lapse is watched by millions.
- Before and after content in trades is as powerful as it is in beauty. Document every transformation.
- Position yourself as the expert. Post about "3 signs you need to replace your roof before it becomes an emergency." Homeowners who consume that content call YOU when the emergency happens.

### The Tate Lens
- The contractor who gives away scope because they don't want conflict is training their clients to take advantage of them.

- Your crew works as hard as you demand. Your standards set their standards. Be on site enough to know if the standards are being met.
- Nobody is going to rescue a contracting business. Not a bank, not a big client, not a lucky break. Systems and relentless execution are the only way out.

## THE MOVES NOBODY TALKS ABOUT

1. **The Milestone Billing Lock:** Never bill on completion. Bill 30% at contract signing, 30% at rough-in or halfway point, 30% at substantial completion, 10% at final walkthrough. This eliminates your cash flow gap and reduces non-payment risk by 80%.

2. **The Maintenance Agreement Engine:** After every job, offer a 1-year maintenance agreement. For HVAC: $150/year for two tune-ups and priority emergency service. This customer stays yours forever and calls you first for every service and replacement need. One truck with 200 maintenance agreements = $30k/year in guaranteed baseline revenue.

3. **The Estimate Follow-Up System:** Send an estimate. 3 days later: "I wanted to make sure you received our proposal and answer any questions." 7 days later: "We have a scheduling opening in 3 weeks — wanted to make sure you had everything you needed to make a decision." This alone increases close rate 30-40%.

4. **The Google Domination Play:** After every completed job, text the client: "It was great working with you. If you're happy with everything, a Google review would mean the world to us — it helps other homeowners find contractors they can trust." Include a direct link. Systematic. Every project. One review a week = 52 reviews a year = top of local search results.

5. **The Builder Relationship:** Get in front of 3-5 local custom home builders. Become their preferred subcontractor. One builder relationship can be 20-30% of your entire revenue — consistent, schedulable, referral-generating work. Builders don't want to find new subs. Be excellent once and you're in forever.

6. **The Commercial Property Manager Audit:** Every commercial building has ongoing maintenance needs. Get in front of 10 property managers with a "preferred vendor" proposal — priority response times, volume pricing, monthly reporting. One contract = $50-200k/year in predictable work.
